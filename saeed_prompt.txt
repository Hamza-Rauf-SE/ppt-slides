Paper I studied:
“Utilizing Pretrained Vision Transformers and Large Language Models for Epileptic Seizure Prediction” by Paras Parani, Umair Mohammad, and Fahad Saeed.

My understanding of the paper:
The paper adapts image and language models for patient-independent seizure prediction from EEG. EEG is multi-channel time-series data, so the study reshapes it into image-like inputs for vision models and text-like tokens for a Longformer model. It trains only selected layers instead of retraining the whole model.

The study uses the lab’s patient-independent MLSPred-Bench benchmarks. Separating training and testing patients makes the evaluation more realistic.

Key results:
- The LLM achieved 79.02% average sensitivity.
- It achieved 70.80% average accuracy.
- It achieved 62.53% average specificity.
- Its average AUC was 71.40%.

What stood out to me:
The paper adapts pretrained models to a very different signal type, uses patient-independent testing, and reports the tradeoff between sensitivity and specificity. This connects directly to my work on efficient fine-tuning and practical model evaluation.

Important limits:
False alarms and specificity still need improvement. Performance changes across prediction windows, and the LLM required much more training time than the vision and ResNet models. A useful next step is to measure accuracy together with model size, speed, memory, power use, and false alarms.

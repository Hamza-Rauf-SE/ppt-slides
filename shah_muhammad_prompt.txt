The main problem in simple words:
1. Solar flares and solar energetic particle (SEP) events can disrupt GPS, radio, satellites, power grids, and astronauts.
2. Magnetic field measurements of solar active regions become multivariate time series. The past 12 hours are used to predict whether a large flare happens in the next 24 hours.
3. Strong flares (M and X class) are rare, so classes are very imbalanced. The lab reports the True Skill Statistic (TSS), hit rate minus false alarm rate, not plain accuracy.
4. The benchmark dataset is SWAN-SF, which he co-authored (Scientific Data, 2020).
5. The pipeline is: clean and process data, build the predictive model, explain the prediction. Long-term goal: near real-time prediction within minutes of new data.

Methods the lab uses:
1. Time series classification with shapelets, sequence models, and feature selection.
2. Functional networks: a graph where nodes are magnetic field parameters and edges show how their time series relate, then graph embedding (his PhD used tensor decomposition for node embedding).
3. Comparing vector, time series, and graph representations (Remote Sensing 2025); preprocessing and sampling effects on flare prediction (ApJS 2024).
4. Synthetic time series generation: AVATAR (SIAM SDM 2025) and TIMED (IEEE ICDM 2025), led by Reza EskandariNasab.
5. Counterfactual explanations for time series, meaning what small change to the input would flip the prediction (SG-CF, CACTUS, DSAA 2025).
6. SEP prediction with multimodal data, ensembles, and shapelets (ApJS and ApJ 2025).
7. LLM work: SolarGPT-QA and Pruning Strategies for Backdoor Defense in LLMs (CIKM 2025), led by Santosh Chapagain.
8. Cyberinfrastructure: FlaPLeT, a no-code web platform for the whole flare prediction workflow.

I am a senior software engineer and AI researcher. For the past two years I have worked as a research assistant to Dr. Umair Khan, listed among Stanford’s World Top 2% Scientists, publishing on compact language models and machine learning surrogates in Q1 journals with impact factors of 8.0 and 5.0.

My research sits where two pressures meet: models are getting larger while the devices and budgets that have to run them are not. My first-author work shows that a 4-bit quantized 4B-parameter model, fine-tuned with QLoRA on synthetic multilingual data, can beat its own 16-bit baseline by 22 percentage points while fitting inside edge memory limits. I care as much about the evaluation as the result: per-class metrics, error analysis, and stating plainly where a study still falls short.

Alongside research, I have spent 6+ years shipping production AI systems: retrieval pipelines, agentic workflows, and document automation used by tens of thousands of people. That experience is why I keep asking what happens to a model after the benchmark table.

I am currently applying to PhD programs, and my interests are broad rather than tied to one narrow topic: machine learning, large language models and vision-language models, efficient and on-device AI, cybersecurity, human-computer interaction, and AI for health and scientific computing. I am happy to shape a direction around the problems a group actually cares about. Get in touch if any of that overlaps with your work.

What I work on

Research interests

My interests are deliberately broad. The thread running through all of them is the same: build a capable model under a real constraint, then test it honestly.

Large language & vision-language models

Adapting LLMs and VLMs to specialised domains through parameter-efficient fine-tuning, structured prompting, retrieval grounding, and reinforcement learning for reasoning.

  • Zero-shot, k-shot, chain-of-thought, and chain-of-verification prompting compared head to head.
  • Multimodal adaptation, from radiological description to document understanding.
  • Agents whose claims are verified by a solver or a real program before they are trusted.

Efficient & on-device AI

Making capable models fit real hardware budgets: parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA and QLoRA), 4-bit and 8-bit quantization, and compact models under strict VRAM ceilings.

  • How much capability does quantization actually cost, and how much can training recover?
  • Privacy as an architectural choice, not a policy afterthought: keep the data on the device.
  • Accuracy, latency, memory, and energy reported together, because only one of them is free.

AI for cybersecurity

Generative models on both sides of the problem: simulating realistic attacks to stress-test defences, and building detectors that run privately at the edge.

  • GAN and VAE based threat simulation, including novel and advanced persistent threats.
  • Multilingual spam and phishing detection on resource-constrained devices.
  • Adversarial robustness, and the secure, compliant deployment of ML services.

Human-computer interaction

Reading indirect human signals under uncertainty, from text and self-reports to gestures and sensor context, and designing interfaces that use those signals responsibly.

  • Fine-grained emotion and behaviour classification where labels are subjective and scarce.
  • Gesture-driven interaction for natural control and accessibility.
  • Interfaces that surface model confidence instead of hiding it behind a single answer.

AI for health & science

Language models in high-stakes clinical settings, and neural surrogates that stand in for numerical solvers too expensive to run at scale.

  • A taxonomy of clinical LLM applications, with safety and fairness as first-class axes.
  • ANN, GPR, and wavelet neural network surrogates for nonlinear transport problems.
  • Medical imaging models adapted on modest hardware rather than a cluster.

Trustworthy evaluation

Comparative benchmarking designed so the comparison is actually fair: one protocol, matched hyperparameters, per-class reporting, and explicit limits.

  • Macro and per-class F1 over headline accuracy, especially on imbalanced classes.
  • Error analysis and failure modes treated as findings, not appendices.
  • Stating plainly where a study falls short, rather than leaving it to the reader.

Complex & Intelligent Systems · Vol. 12, art. 119 · 2026

Efficient multilingual spam detection on resource-constrained devices: a comparative analysis of QLoRA fine-tuning of Gemma 3, Qwen 3, and Llama 3.2 models

Hamza Rauf, Umair Khan, Jomana A. Bashatah, Aurang Zaib

A privacy-preserving framework for multilingual spam detection where data and compute are both scarce. I synthesised a balanced 21-language corpus by high-fidelity translation of the English Enron set, then fine-tuned 4-bit quantized Gemma 3-4B, Qwen 3-4B, and Llama 3.2-3B under one shared QLoRA protocol. Gemma 3-4B reached 90% accuracy and 0.88 F1, which is 22 percentage points above its own 16-bit baseline, while quantization cut every model’s VRAM footprint by more than half, bringing two of the three under a 4 GB edge-memory target.

Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence · 2025

Comparative analysis of advanced machine learning models for second-grade liquid via stimulating CoFe2O4 nanoparticles over a convective moving plate

Umair Khan, Hamza Rauf, Aurang Zaib, Abeer M. Alotaibi

Time-dependent flow of a magnetized second-grade nanofluid is expensive to solve numerically, so we benchmarked machine learning surrogates against a high-fidelity solver. Gaussian Process Regression matched the ANN-Levenberg-Marquardt benchmark at R² = 1.0 while additionally quantifying its own uncertainty, a property that matters more than a decimal place of accuracy when the surrogate feeds engineering design decisions.

Asia-Pacific Journal of Chemical Engineering · August 2026

Residual strength prediction of chemically reactive two-phase nanofluid flow in a Bingham-Papanastasiou rheological theory using a Morlet-based wavelet neural network approach

Umair Khan, Hamza Rauf, Aurang Zaib, J. K. Madhukesh

Chemically reactive two-phase Bingham-Papanastasiou nanofluid flow under magnetic effects, velocity slip, and zero mass-flux conditions. Numerical solutions from MATLAB bvp4c trained Morlet wavelet neural networks under both Levenberg-Marquardt and Adam optimisation. The Levenberg-Marquardt model tracked the numerical results closely and produced lower prediction error than the Adam-trained network, supporting Morlet wavelet networks as efficient surrogates for complex nanofluid behaviour.
